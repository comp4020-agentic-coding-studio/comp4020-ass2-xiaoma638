# Cancel and retry

SLOP2805 · Week 9 · Studio framing

Cancel is the only control a user presses to make something **not** happen.

Which makes it the only one whose correctness they can never observe.

---

## Where we were, and where we are going

**Week 8** gave the system's failures a vocabulary. **This week** is the user's
two actions, and what they are owed.

By 17:00 you can:

- write a guarantee: one sentence, about their data, that **can be false**
- implement cancel and retry so it holds on the awkward paths
- write a test that asserts a guarantee rather than current behaviour

---

## The situation

A user cancels at 24 of 40 MB. Button greys out, bar disappears, page says
**Cancelled**.

Two completely different realities are consistent with that screen:

- the 24 MB were discarded
- the 24 MB sit in a server-side buffer that a scheduled job assembles in four
  hours

The user cannot tell. They may find out from a storage bill.

---

## A guarantee is a sentence that can fail

> ✗ **"Cancel stops the upload."** — true of any implementation, including one
> that leaks. Cannot fail, so it is not a guarantee.

> ✓ **"Cancel: no bytes from this attempt are retained anywhere after the button
> returns."**

> ✓ **"Retry: resumes from the last stage the server acknowledged, and re-sends
> nothing before it."**

Both can be false. That is what makes them testable.

---

## The three awkward paths

**Cancel during a stall** — no progress event for thirty seconds. What are you
cancelling?

**Cancel between last chunk and acknowledgement** — the operation may already
have succeeded.

**Retry after a timeout** — the server may still be working on attempt one.
Week 8's timeout row, arriving with consequences.

Each is a place where the honest guarantee is **weaker** than the one you would
like to make. Weakening the sentence is the correct move.

---

## Worked example — the test that can break

```js
// Guarantee: no bytes are retained after cancel() returns.
test.each(["transferring", "stalled", "awaiting-ack"])("from %s", async (from) => {
  const sim = new Upload(); await sim.driveTo(from); await sim.cancel();
  expect(sim.retainedBytes).toBe(0);            // ✓ the guarantee
});
```

Compare the test that protects a bug — it passes now and **fails the day you fix
it**:

```js
expect(sim.retainedBytes).toBe(24_000_000);     // ✗ today's behaviour
```

```comment
`Upload` is the shipped course prototype's simulated client; no network is involved.
```

## Your turn — the build

1. **Two guarantees**, one sentence each, as a comment **above** each handler,
   written **before** the handler.
2. **Cancel and retry** that keep them, from all three awkward paths.
3. **One test per guarantee**, reaching its assertion by at least two entry
   states.
4. **Pre-commitment text** in the interface: *"Cancel (nothing is kept)"*,
   *"Retry from 24 MB"* — readable before the button is pressed.

Then swap prototypes and try to falsify your partner's sentences.

---

## The mistake to expect

Fixing the sentence instead of the code, silently.

When the pair swap breaks your guarantee you have two honest moves — **change
the code**, or **weaken the sentence in writing**. Both are legitimate. Leaving
the pair disagreeing is not.

Also expect one state in your week 2 model with **no defined cancel behaviour**.
It is usually a verifying state, and it is usually the one nobody considered.

---

## What this week cannot tell you

Whether the guarantee is the one the user **wanted**.

"Retry resumes from stage 3" may be excellent and still wrong for someone on a
metered connection who would rather be asked.

And a limit you cannot cross from here: your guarantees are about a simulator you
control. On a real service, cancellation is a **request**, not an instruction —
the truthful sentence would be weaker again.

---

## What leaves the room

- two written guarantees and the code that keeps them
- two tests that assert a claim, not a behaviour
- pre-commitment text on both controls

**Where it goes:** *"Cancel and retry keep the guarantees written above their
handlers"* is a spec line of **Assignment 3**, and a marker will read your
sentence and then try to break it.
