## The taxonomy, by what is still possible

| Failure | Connection | Server may still be working | Resumes itself | Safe to retry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stall** | up | yes | possibly | not yet |
| **Timeout** | up | **yes, unknown** | no — you stopped | only if idempotent |
| **Disconnection** | gone | unknown | no | from last ack |

---

## The timeout row is the one that catches people

A timeout is a decision **you** made about how long to wait.

It says nothing about the server, which may be thirty seconds into a two-minute
virus scan and about to succeed.

> An interface that reports a timeout as **"Upload failed"** has asserted
> something it does not know.

---

## Detection is not estimation

```js
// stall: one timestamp, no rate, no prediction
if (now - lastProgressEvent > STALL_AFTER_MS) enter("stalled");
```

That is the whole mechanism. No estimator is involved, which is why week 5's
estimators could stay simple.

**Two clean mechanisms beat one clever one.** Keep them apart.

---

## Worked example — write the message first

The method, and it is not a style preference. Writing the handler first gives you
messages that describe code.

> ✗ **"Request aborted with status 0."**
>
> ✗ **"Connection lost."** — one part out of three
>
> ✓ **"The connection dropped after 24 of 40 MB. Nothing has been saved yet.
> Retry will resume from the last completed chunk."**

| Part | The sentence answers |
| --- | --- |
| What happened | the connection dropped after 24 of 40 MB |
| What it means for the file | nothing has been saved yet |
| What happens next | retry resumes from the last completed chunk |

```comment
24 of 40 MB is the shipped simulator's disconnection injector, not a real transfer.
```

## The mistake to expect

Leaving the shimmer on.

A bar that keeps animating through a stall is asserting liveness that is false.
It is **week 1's exact failure**, now in your own code, and it is the one thing
that fails *Behaviour under abnormal conditions* outright in Assignment 3.

The check: cover the first clause of each message. Does the rest still tell the
user something about their file? If not, you wrote two parts, not three.

---

## What this week cannot tell you

Whether the messages are **understood**.

You wrote them, which makes you the worst available judge: you already hold the
thing the sentence is trying to convey.

Neighbours breaking each other's states tests **correctness** — does the message
match the state — not comprehension. **Week 11** is the first time anyone reads
one cold, and *stall* is the state they will be shown.
