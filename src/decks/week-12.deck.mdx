# The last percent

SLOP2805 · Week 12 · Showcase briefing

Eight minutes. Three paths, live.

Then two challenges on a claim you chose to keep.

---

## Where we were, and where we are going

Twelve weeks ago you watched a bar sit at 99% for eleven seconds and then fail.
You could say something was wrong with it and not much more.

Today you show an interface that does not do that — and you take questions about
the parts of it you **could not** make honest.

There is nothing new to build. The written kit is due tomorrow.

---

## Why a defence and not a presentation

Everything else in this course is marked on artefacts you control.

A defence tests the one thing an artefact cannot show: **whether you know why.**

> A well-built interface whose maker cannot say which of its numbers are invented
> is a weaker outcome than a rougher one whose maker can.

---

## The eight minutes

| Time | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **~4 min** | drive the deployed site through a **success**, a **stall**, and one **failure**, using the exposed controls |
| **~1 min** | name one `kept with reason` row, its reason, and its cost |
| **~3 min** | two challenges from the room |

Live, on the deployed URL. Not `localhost`. Not a recording.

---

## Three answers that score

> **"It holds."** — that string comes from the acknowledgement event, here.

> **"It doesn't hold."** — I kept it because of X, and it costs Y.

> **"I don't know."** — and here is how I would find out.

The third is a **full-credit answer**. It is the sentence week 11 spent an
afternoon teaching.

---

## One answer that does not

> **"It felt better."**

Not because preference is illegitimate — week 6 was an entire studio on
preference — but because

> *"I preferred it and I have no evidence"*

is available, true, and takes the same breath.

The room has been trained to call it since week 1.

---

## Worked example — a defence that earns the marks

**Claim:** the verification state is held on screen for 400 ms when it lasts ~80 ms.

> **Challenge:** "So the interface is lying about a duration."
>
> **Answer:** "Yes, by up to 320 ms, and the register says so. I kept it because
> at 80 ms the state is unreadable — it reads as a flicker, and two testers in
> week 11 asked what had flashed. The cost is that anyone comparing it with the
> elapsed counter sees them disagree. If I had another week I would hold it only
> when the stage is shorter than 200 ms, rather than always."

Names the deception, the evidence, the cost, and the thing not done.

```comment
The 80 ms verify stage is the shipped simulator's; the tester reaction is an
illustration written for this slide, not a recorded observation.
```

## Your turn — choose the uncomfortable row

Pick the `kept with reason` row you genuinely find hard to defend.

A safe choice produces a safe two minutes and a low *Defence* mark. The room is
not hostile and nobody is trying to catch you out — the criterion rewards the
**difficulty of the question you volunteered for**.

---

## The mistake to expect

Rehearsing the happy path.

A demo that only shows a success has not shown the course. The three paths are
in the spec line, and a stall you cannot trigger on demand is a stall you cannot
claim to handle.

Second: demoing from `localhost` because the deploy is slow. The deployed URL is
what the criterion names, and base-path bugs only appear there.

---

## What this week cannot tell you

Whether you were **right**.

A defence establishes that you can say why, from evidence, and that you know
which decisions are unsupported. It does not establish they were good.

What you take away is smaller and more portable: you can look at any waiting
interface — including one you are about to build at work — and say what it
claims, what could support each claim, and which parts were invented to fill a
silence.
