# Motion, and how long it felt

SLOP2805 · Week 6 · Studio framing

Does how the bar moves change how long the wait feels?

And — the part that transfers — **how would you know?**

---

## What the published work actually measured

Harrison and colleagues at Carnegie Mellon ran forced-choice comparisons between
animations of **identical real duration**, with participants who did not know the
durations matched.

- the measure was **perceived duration**
- reported effects sit in the range of **a few per cent** of perceived time
- it is not a claim about trust, comprehension, or whether a number is better

Their summary page is this week's reading. Read the effect sizes, not the
headline.

## What twenty classmates can reproduce

The **procedure**. Not the conditions. Five reasons, named now so the write-up
is not hindsight:

1. **Not blind** — you built the treatments and know which you expect to win
2. **Order effects** — whoever sees the third has already waited a minute
3. **Demand characteristics** — they know it is a week about animation
4. **No stakes** — nobody is waiting for a file they need
5. **n ≈ 20** against an effect of a few per cent

## Worked example — the assertion that makes it valid

Three easings, one duration. The difference is invisible by eye, so assert it:

```js
const TREATMENTS = { linear: t => t,
                     decelerate: t => 1 - (1 - t) ** 2,
                     pulsed: t => t + 0.02 * Math.sin(t * 18 * Math.PI) };

// A treatment 400ms longer than another invalidates the whole exercise.
for (const ease of Object.values(TREATMENTS)) {
  console.assert(Math.abs(ease(1) - 1) < 1e-9, "must finish at exactly 1");
}
```

Easing changes the **shape** of the journey, never its length. If `ease(1) !== 1`
you are comparing durations, not motions.

## The mistake to expect

Dropping a response.

Someone ranks the pulsed bar fastest "because they weren't paying attention".
The temptation to exclude them is the exercise.

> If you were tempted to drop a response, **write the temptation down instead**.
> That sentence is worth more than the cleaned table.

Second mistake: writing "small sample size". Specific beats generic —
*"n ≈ 20 against a published effect of a few per cent"* is a reason; the other
is a phrase.

## What this week cannot tell you

Almost anything about perceived duration. That is the intended outcome.

If your three came out in the published order, that is **not replication** —
with this design it is close to a coin landing the way you expected.

What you get is calibration: you now know from the inside what a weak result
feels like while you are collecting it.
