---

## A claim is anything the interface asserts

- a **number** — "99%", "about 4 minutes remaining"
- a **word** — "Finalising", "Almost done", "Complete"
- a **motion** — a bar that accelerates is claiming something about rate
- an **absence** — a screen showing nothing claims nothing needs saying

If a reader can take something from it, it is a claim.

---

## A source event is something the process *reports*

Not something it does. Something it emits, at a time you could write down.

| the process does | the process reports |
| --- | --- |
| writes bytes to a socket | `progress: 4.2 MB of 11 MB` |
| waits on a server lock | *(nothing)* |
| finishes durably | `committed` |

The middle row is where progress bars are invented.

---

## Three verdicts, this week only

- `sourced` — an event exists that could support it
- `unsourceable` — nothing the process emits could support it
- `not determinable from outside` — you are out here, and you cannot tell

In **week 10** you do this to your own code, and the third verdict is taken
away.

## Which is the point

A claim about a failure you cannot reproduce is not a claim anybody can check.

The simulator is not a convenience. It is what makes the next eleven weeks
falsifiable.

---

## Worked example — four rows of a claims table

Simulated case, replayed from the course prototype: 40 MB, 99% in nine seconds,
then eleven seconds of nothing, then **Upload failed**.

| Claim | Possible source event | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "99%" | `progress` with `loaded/total` | `sourced` |
| bar still shimmering during the 11 s | *none — no event fires in that window* | `unsourceable` |
| reaching 99% in 9 s implies ~0.1 s left | derived from the bar's own rate, not reported | `unsourceable` |
| "Upload failed" | a server response we cannot see | `not determinable from outside` |

The shimmer is the interesting row. It is the only claim on that screen that was
**false for eleven seconds**, and it is made without a word.

## The mistake to expect

A table with only text in it.

Motion and absence are claims, and a table that lists three strings and no
movement is an incomplete table. It is the commonest way **Assignment 1** loses
marks on *Evidence tracing* — 40 of its 100.

The other one: reaching for `unsourceable` when the honest verdict is
`not determinable from outside`. From out here you cannot tell a lie from a
shortcut from a constraint you cannot see.

---

## What this week cannot tell you

Whether any of these interfaces is **wrong**.

An unsourceable claim is not automatically a bad one. A designer may have had an
excellent reason to show a number nobody can justify.

You are recording what is claimed and what could support it. Judgement comes
later, and it comes with evidence.
