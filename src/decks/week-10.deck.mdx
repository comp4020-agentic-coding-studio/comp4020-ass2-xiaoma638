# Numbers without sources

SLOP2805 · Week 10 · Studio framing

Week 1, turned inward.

**Which of the promises your interface makes can you actually keep?**

---

## Where we were, and where we are going

In **week 1** you stood outside an interface you did not build and asked what
*could* support what it said.

Today you have the source, your state model, your denominator, your estimator
and its measured instability, and every failure on demand.

So the question sharpens — and the verdicts change with it.

---

## What changed since week 1

| Week 1 | Week 10 |
| --- | --- |
| `sourced` — some event could support it | `holds` — **this** event does, and you can name the line |
| `unsourceable` — nothing could | `removed` — nothing does, so it is gone from the screen |
| `not determinable from outside` | **withdrawn — you are inside now** |

Losing the third verdict is the week working. *"I cannot tell"* was honest in
week 1 and is an evasion today.

---

## The verdict week 1 had no use for

**`kept with reason`** — a claim with no source event that you are displaying
anyway, knowingly.

A rounded figure. A minimum display duration. A reassuring phrase during
verification.

That can be a good decision. It stops being one the moment it is unexamined.

---

## Three near-misses that are not source events

- **a value from a timer.** A bar advancing on `setInterval` reports that time
  passed, while appearing to report that work happened.
- **a constant measured once,** on your machine, in week 5. Evidence then. A
  hardcoded number now, unless the code re-measures.
- **a library default** that arrived and was never chosen.

---

## Worked example — one row, three ways

| Rendered | Source event | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Verifying…" held 400 ms | `verify:start`, but the state lasts ~80 ms | **kept with reason** |

> The verification state completes in under 80 ms. A state that flashes for
> 80 ms is harder to read than one held for 400 ms, so this state is displayed
> **longer than it exists**. The cost is that a reader comparing it with the
> elapsed counter will see them disagree by up to 320 ms.

Names the deception, the justification, **and the price**. A marker may still
disagree — a reason you can lose an argument about is still a reason.

> ✗ "It looks better." ✗ "Users expect it." ✗ "It felt too abrupt."

```comment
The 80 ms figure is the shipped simulator's verify stage, not a measured service.
```

## The mistake to expect

Going straight to the suspicious number.

The claims you are least likely to examine are the ones you wrote so early you no
longer see them. Most people's worst row is something added in **week 2** and
never looked at again.

Second mistake: marking a row `removed` in the register while it is still on the
screen. The register describes the deployed interface, not your intentions.

---

## What this week cannot tell you

Whether the claims that survive are **understood**.

Every number now has an event behind it. Nothing establishes that a reader takes
from it what you meant.

*"Resumes from stage 3"* is impeccably sourced and means nothing to somebody who
has never seen your stage names. That gap is **week 11**.
