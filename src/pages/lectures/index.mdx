This course has four lectures.

That is a decision, not an oversight. Most of what this subject teaches is
learned by building the same interface badly and then less badly, and a lecture
cannot do that. So the four that remain each carry something you could not
arrive at on your own at a keyboard: a vocabulary, a distinction, a standard, or
a limit on what your evidence can support.

Each runs Tuesdays, 11:00&ndash;12:00, and the
[studio](/sessions/) two days later applies it. The
other eight weeks open with a short framing in the studio instead &mdash; named
on each week's page, with the reading that goes with it.

| Week | Lecture | What it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [What 99% Promises](/lectures/week-01/) | claim, source event, verdict &mdash; the move used every week after |
| 4 | [Saying Nothing, Precisely](/lectures/week-04/) | the three honest registers when there is no denominator |
| 7 | [Reading an Interface Without Looking at It](/lectures/week-07/) | status as text and semantics; the standard Assignment 2 is marked against |
| 11 | [Three People Is Not a Study](/lectures/week-11/) | what a handful of observations can and cannot establish |

Week 1 carries the [deck](/decks/week-01/).
