Most of these words are in ordinary use. This course uses them in a narrower
sense, and the narrowing is usually the teaching. Where a term is this course's
own coinage rather than standard vocabulary, it says so.

## claim

Anything a reader can take from a waiting interface. Numbers and words are the
obvious ones; **motion, rate and absence** are claims too. A bar that shimmers
asserts the process is alive. A screen with no error asserts there is nothing to
report. Introduced in [week 1](/sessions/01-what-99-percent-promises/).

## source event

Something the process **reports**, at a moment you could write down — not
something the process *does*. A server writing bytes to disk is an activity;
`progress: 4.2 MB of 11 MB` is a source event. Only the second can support a
claim on a screen, and the gap between the two is where invented progress lives.

*Course term.* Standard vocabulary would say "telemetry" or just "an event"; the
narrowing is the insistence that it be reported and timestamped.

## verdict

The judgement pairing a claim with its source. Outside a system you have three:
`sourced`, `unsourceable`, `not determinable from outside`. Inside your own code
([week 10](/sessions/10-numbers-without-sources/)) the third is withdrawn and
replaced by **kept with reason**.

## kept with reason

A claim with no source event that you are displaying anyway, knowingly, with a
written justification naming the deception, the reason and its cost. A rounded
figure or a minimum display duration is usually this. Distinguished from an
excuse by whether a reader could argue with it: *"it looks better"* is not a
reason.

*Course term.*

## denominator

The bottom of the fraction a percentage is. Interfaces are careful about the
numerator and careless about the denominator, which is how a bar can be accurate
and useless at once. Every percentage on a screen should be able to say what it
is a percentage **of**, and what that excludes.
[Week 3](/sessions/03-where-is-the-denominator/).

## determinate / indeterminate

Whether a denominator exists. The platform is explicit about this: a
`ProgressEvent` carries `lengthComputable`, and the `<progress>` element spells
indeterminate by omitting `value` — which is deliberately not the same as
`value="0"`.

## liveness

The weakest honest thing an interface can say: *something is still happening*.
Asserts nothing about position, duration, or eventual success. Requires a
heartbeat event; a looping animation is not one.
[Week 4](/sessions/04-no-idea-how-long/).

## elapsed

*You have been waiting this long.* A fact about the user rather than about the
process, which is why it is the only register that cannot be wrong — and the one
that makes a wait feel longest. That trade is not resolvable and the course does
not pretend otherwise.

## named stage

*This particular thing is happening now.* Requires the state model from
[week 2](/sessions/02-what-counts-as-done/), and says nothing about how many
stages remain.

## fractional reading

What a reader can take as a ratio from a display containing no numbers. A
spinner that slows down reads as "nearly there"; a three-dot animation on its
third dot reads as two-thirds. An indeterminate display that admits a fractional
reading has made a determinate claim with indeterminate data.

*Course term.*

## state

A claim about the process, as opposed to a **phase**, which is a claim about the
display. "The bar is at 60%" is a phase. "The final chunk is acknowledged and we
are waiting on durability confirmation" is a state. You can pass through several
states in one phase, which is how an interface says nothing true for eleven
seconds.

## completion criterion

The one sentence naming what has to be true before the interface may say
*complete*. "The last byte left this machine", "the service acknowledged the
final chunk" and "the object is durable" are three different criteria, seconds
apart, and only the last survives a power cut.

## instability

How much a displayed estimate contradicts itself: the mean absolute change in
the shown value between consecutive refreshes, in seconds. Deliberately **not**
a measure of accuracy — it measures what the user experiences. An estimator can
be more accurate and more annoying. [Week 5](/sessions/05-why-the-estimate-jumps/).

## guarantee

A one-sentence, **falsifiable** statement of what an action promises about a
user's data. "Cancel stops the upload" cannot fail and is therefore not a
guarantee. "No bytes from this attempt are retained after the button returns"
can fail, which is what makes it testable.
[Week 9](/sessions/09-cancel-and-retry/).

## existence proof / rate

*At least one person read the stall as a failure* is established by one
observation. *Most people read the stall as a failure* is not established by
three, or thirty. Small-n observation yields the first and never the second, and
conflating them is the most common overreach in a write-up.
[Week 11](/sessions/11-watching-someone-wait/).

## stall, timeout, disconnection

Three ways a transfer stops without finishing, distinguished by what can still
be happening. A **stall** has a live connection and may resume. A **timeout** is
a decision *you* made; the server may still be working and succeeding. A
**disconnection** means the transport is gone. Telling a user the wrong one
makes them do the wrong thing. [Week 8](/sessions/08-stuck-timed-out-disconnected/).
