Course-wide rules, so the briefs can link to one place instead of restating
them. Anything not covered here is covered by Slop University's general
coursework rules.

## Extensions

Ask before the deadline. A short extension — up to five days — needs a sentence
saying what happened and does not need a document. Longer than that, or after
the deadline has passed, goes through the University's standard process.

The reason short extensions are easy is that this course's deadlines are
deliberately placed to be movable: none of the three assessments blocks the next
week's teaching, and [Assignment 2](/assessments/waiting-interface-prototype/) is
due on a day with no class precisely so that a late submission does not cost you
a studio.

## Academic integrity

The ordinary rule applies: the work you submit is yours, and anything that is
not yours is attributed.

Two things worth being explicit about, because this course invites both:

**Working together is expected in the studio.** The pair audits in
[week 7](/sessions/07-without-the-animation/) and the observation rotation in
[week 11](/sessions/11-watching-someone-wait/) are collaborative by design. What
you submit afterwards is your own analysis of what happened. If a classmate
found the bug, say so — "my partner broke my cancel guarantee by cancelling
mid-stall" is a stronger sentence than one that quietly takes the credit, and it
is marked as such.

**Generated code is allowed and must be declared.** Say what you used it for, in
a line. The thing this course actually assesses — whether you can say what your
interface claims and what supports each claim — is not made easier by generating
the interface, and is usually made harder, because you end up defending
decisions you did not make.

## The week 11 observation: consent and data

[Week 11](/sessions/11-watching-someone-wait/) has students observe each other
using their prototypes. The arrangements are fixed and not negotiable by
individual students or tutors.

**Participation is voluntary, in both roles.** You may decline to be observed,
to observe, or both, for any reason, and you do not have to give one. Tell a
tutor before the session and you are given the
[structured walkthrough](/sessions/11-watching-someone-wait/) instead. It is
graded identically, on the same criterion, and it is not a lesser task.

**Consent is read aloud before each round**, from a supplied script, and a
participant may stop at any point without explanation. Stopping mid-round is not
a failed session; it is a recorded outcome.

**What is recorded.** Handwritten notes only: what the participant did, and
what they said, in their words. **No audio, no video, no screen recording, and
no names.** Record sheets identify participants as P1, P2, P3.

**Where it goes.** Record sheets are the student's own; they are quoted in the
[Assignment 3](/assessments/honest-waiting-kit/) evaluation record and are not
collected by the course. Nothing from the session is published, and a quoted
line may not be attributed to an identifiable person.

**Marking does not depend on participating.** The criterion asks whether your
conclusions are proportionate to your evidence. It does not ask how many people
you watched, or whether you watched anybody.

## What the prototype may and may not do

The course prototype, `upload-ui`, simulates every transfer locally. That is a
requirement, not a convenience, and it has a policy dimension:

- **No student prototype uploads to a real service.** Not to the University's,
  not to a third party's, not to your own. The simulator is the only backend, all
  semester.
- **No real personal files as test data.** The file picker accepts a real file so
  the interaction is honest, but the prototype never reads its contents — only
  its reported size. If your prototype reads bytes, that is a bug and a policy
  breach at once.
- **Demonstration data is labelled.** The supplied traces are recorded sample
  data shipped with the course, and anything built on them says so. The
  [progress simulator](/simulator/) on this site carries the same label.

## Getting help

Ask in the studio first — that is what three hours with two tutors is for. Email
is for things that cannot wait until Thursday. Anything you think is wrong with
this site, including a broken link or a page that contradicts another page, is
worth reporting and will be fixed rather than defended.
