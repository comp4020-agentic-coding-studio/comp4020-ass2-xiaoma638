There is a body of published work claiming that the shape of a progress
animation changes perceived duration. You are not going to take its word for it,
and you are not going to be able to replicate it either. Both halves of that
sentence are the lesson.

## In the room

Thursday 1 April, 14:00–17:00. No lecture; the studio opens with a 25-minute
framing on perceived duration, what the published studies actually measured,
and what a room of twenty classmates can and cannot reproduce.

## What you build

Three treatments — linear, decelerating, pulsed — over waits of **identical**
duration. The class ranks them without being told the durations match. You
record the result exactly as it came out, then write down why it is weak: n≈20,
order effects, unblinded, and participants who know what the exercise is testing.

## What this week cannot tell you

Almost anything, and that is the point. This is the week you learn what a weak
result looks like from the inside, so that in
[week 11](/sessions/11-watching-someone-wait/) you can tell the difference.
